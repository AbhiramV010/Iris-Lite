02 · Detection

Deciding something happened

Three independent detectors, each with its own idea of what counts. Any one of them can raise an event on its own, none of them can veto another, and all three analyse at 640×360 and cap themselves at 15 fps so that the four cores have room left for the camera and the buffer.

What they have in common

Every detector attaches read-only to iris_live_frame, copies the current frame, downscales it, and runs its own loop. When one decides an event has occurred it builds a CaptureClass, pads the window by five seconds at each end, stamps the current GPIO state onto it, and sends it to the buffer manager over the event bus.

The five-second pad matters more than it looks. A detector needs time to become confident — fifty consecutive frames of motion, two seconds of sustained ground contact — so by the time it fires, the interesting part is already in the past. The pad reaches back into the ring and recovers it.

Two-tier motion filter

The premise is that most frames are boring and proving it should be nearly free. Tier one runs on every frame and is allowed to be wrong often. Tier two is careful, and only ever sees frames tier one has already flagged.

Tier one — the cheap pass

Two signals, either of which is enough to promote a frame:

  • Background subtraction. OpenCV's CNT subtractor, chosen because it is the fastest of the bgsegm family on ARM. Fires when more than 10% of the mask is non-zero.
  • Luminance shift. An exponential moving average of mean frame brightness with α = 0.05, firing when the current frame departs from the average by more than 90 grey levels.
triggered = abs(curr - last_avg_lum) > LUM_THRESH
last_avg_lum = (ALPHA * curr) + ((1 - ALPHA) * last_avg_lum)

mask = fgbg.apply(gray)
motion = (cv2.countNonZero(mask) / (640 * 480)) > SENSITIVITY

return (triggered or motion), gray, mask

A light switch and a person both clear this bar, and that is the intended behaviour. Tier one is a filter, not a decision.

Tier two — entropy and persistence

Promoted frames go through three checks in order, and failing any one of them ends the frame's candidacy:

  1. Shannon entropy of the moving pixels. The pixel values under the motion mask are histogrammed and their entropy computed. Below 3.0, the change is rejected as flat — a cloud crossing the sun changes every pixel by the same amount, and carries almost no information.
  2. Centroid travel. The largest contour's bounding-box centre must move more than 20 px between frames. Movement increments a persistence counter; anything smaller decrements it.
  3. Persistence. Fifty qualifying frames raise the event. At 15 fps that is a little over three seconds of continuous, directional movement. 
if last_centroid:
    dist = sqrt((cx - lx)**2 + (cy - ly)**2)
    if dist > 20:
        persistence_count += 1
    else:
        persistence_count = max(0, persistence_count - 1)

if persistence_count >= 50:
    # raise CaptureClass(trigger="tiered_cap", duration=10.0)

The decay on the counter is what rejects a tree in wind. A branch oscillates around a fixed point, so its centroid moves and returns; the counter climbs and falls and never reaches fifty. A person walking accumulates monotonically. Losing tier one entirely resets both the counter and the last known centroid to zero.

Quirk

The motion ratio divides by 640 * 480 while the analysed frame is 640×360. The mask only has 230,400 pixels, so the nominal 10% threshold behaves like about 13.3% of the real frame. The detector was tuned with this in place, so the constant and the divisor are effectively one calibrated value rather than two independent ones.

Zone monitor

This detector answers a narrower question than the motion filter: is someone standing somewhere they shouldn't be. It runs in two phases.

Phase one — learning the zone, once

At startup it takes a single frame, converts to HSV, and thresholds for grass: 

grassRange = cv2.inRange(hsv,
                         np.array([25,  30,  20]),
                         np.array([95, 255, 255]))

The resulting mask is dilated and morphologically opened and closed to remove speckle, then contoured. Rather than keeping the largest region alone, defineZone keeps the largest contour plus whichever of the next five has the nearest centroid to it — a lawn split by a path reads as two blobs, and taking only the bigger one would leave half the garden unwatched.

This happens exactly once. The zone is fixed for the lifetime of the process, which means the detector inherits whatever the lighting was at startup.

Phase two — watching for feet

The steady-state loop uses GSOC background subtraction, a slower and more accurate subtractor than tier one's CNT, tuned conservatively:

ParameterValueEffect
nSamples20Background samples held per pixel
replaceRate0.003How readily a sample is replaced — low, so a parked object takes a long time to become background
propagationRate0.01How readily a background decision spreads to neighbours
hitsThreshold32Matches needed before a pixel is treated as background

From the foreground mask it keeps the three largest contours, and from each of those only the bottom 20 pixels of the bounding box. The reasoning is that what matters is where something is standing, not how tall it is — a person leaning over the fence overlaps the lawn in the mask, but their feet do not.

Overlap between that strip and the zone mask is measured in pixels and averaged over a rolling ten-frame window, which gives the detector hysteresis:

Enter
Average overlap rises to 3 px or more — start the clock
Exit
Average overlap falls below 1 px — stop the clock
Fire
The interval between the two exceeded 2 seconds

Separate enter and exit thresholds stop a subject hovering at the boundary from producing a burst of events. The event is tagged Grass Overlap and reports its true measured duration rather than a fixed ten seconds.

Audio classifier

Sound reaches around corners and through the dark, which makes it a useful complement to two detectors that both need line of sight.

The volume gate

Audio arrives at 16 kHz in 4,096-sample chunks. Every chunk is appended to a five-minute ring in shared memory and its RMS amplitude computed. Below an RMS of 0.08 — roughly 72 dB SPL — the model is never invoked. Running a CNN against silence forty times a second is exactly the kind of always-on cost the whole project exists to avoid, and it is why the listener idles at under 1% CPU.

Feature extraction

Above the gate, the last two seconds are turned into a 128-band log-mel spectrogram. The mel filterbank and the STFT are written out longhand in NumPy rather than pulled from librosa — the Pi image does not need to carry a large audio library for one function — then normalised to [0, 1] and padded or cropped to exactly 98 time steps.

The model

A small convolutional network trained from scratch on Freesound data for this project. It is not a fine-tuned YAMNet or any other pre-trained audio backbone. 

models.Sequential([
    layers.Input(shape=(128, 98, 1)),
    layers.Conv2D(32, (3, 3), activation='relu'),
    layers.MaxPooling2D((2, 2)),
    layers.Conv2D(64, (3, 3), activation='relu'),
    layers.MaxPooling2D((2, 2)),
    layers.Flatten(),
    layers.Dense(64, activation='relu'),
    layers.Dropout(0.3),
    layers.Dense(8, activation='softmax'),
])

The trained Keras H5 was too large to commit, so it ships as a converted TFLite file and runs through a LiteRT interpreter. That conversion is why the model is on the repository at all, and it also happens to be the right format for the Pi.

From prediction to event

A concerning class above 45% confidence opens a detection rather than firing immediately. While it stays open the listener tracks the peak confidence seen; when the classification falls back to ambience or below threshold, the detection closes and one event is emitted covering the whole span.

A four-second scream therefore produces one clip with the loudest, most confident moment recorded in its trigger string — Screaming (81.3%) — not sixty overlapping events. The trigger string is the only place in the system where a confidence figure survives.

GPIO sensors

A reed switch on BCM pin 17 and a PIR motion sensor on BCM pin 27 never raise events by themselves. They are context: their state is stamped into every CaptureClass so that downstream code knows a door was open while the clip was being recorded.

Both are read through sensor_helper.py, which uses a falling-edge interrupt with a 200 ms debounce and reports true if the pin fired within the last ten seconds. Polling a pin at the moment an event is built would almost always miss the edge that mattered; the ten-second window is what makes the reading useful.

Cross-platform

The whole module is wrapped in a platform.system() == "Linux" check. On Windows or macOS the GPIO functions are simply never defined, the calls fall through the detectors' bare try/except blocks, and the system runs with the sensor fields unset. Development on a desktop does not require stubbing anything out.
